# 01 · The original career and its scenarios

> **Research for the drawing board (owner, 2026-11-04). Not a decision.** It grounds the redesign in the original
> game: how one career runs, what each of the fifteen scenarios adds, how uma-racer maps them today, a candidate
> mini-game per scenario, and the pacing arithmetic once nothing runs on a real-time clock. Numbers are the community's
> measurements of the original (JP unless marked). They are descriptive notes, not a data import.

## 1. How a career works

**The calendar.**
- A career covers three school years: Junior, Classic and Senior. Each month has an early and a late turn, so a year
  is 24 turns and a career **72**, plus a finale. URA's finale adds about six turns (three races with training
  between), so roughly 78 in all.
- The debut race falls in late June of Junior year, after about 11 pre-debut turns.
- **Summer camp** (early July to late August of Classic and Senior, four turns each) runs every facility at its top
  level.
- Fixed events: New Year (pick stats, energy or SP) and inspiration in early April, both in Classic and Senior.
- A hand-played career takes **20–40 minutes**, 15–30 seconds a turn.

**A turn is one command:** train at one of five facilities, rest, recreation, infirmary (only with a bad condition),
or race. Buying skills takes no turn.

**Training.**
- Each facility raises one main stat and one or two side stats, adds 4–5 SP, and costs about 20 energy. Wit restores
  5 instead.
- At URA's level 1, Speed training pays about 11 Speed and 6 Power for −21 energy.
- A facility goes up a level every four uses, from 1 to 5, and pays about a third more at 5.
- Gains are multiplied by mood, by each support card present (+5% each, plus its training bonuses), by friendship and
  by the trainee's growth rates.

**Energy and mood.**
- Energy runs 0–100, and events can raise the maximum.
- The failure chance rises as energy falls (players rest at 15–25%), and a failure costs stats and mood.
- Rest restores 30, 50 or 70 energy, usually 50. Recreation raises mood. The infirmary usually cures a condition.
- Mood has five steps, Awful to Great, worth −20% to +20% on training.
- Three or more races in a row risk a bad condition.

**Races.**
- Each trainee has dated **objectives** that follow her real horse's career: her debut, then specific races or fan
  targets. Missing one ends the run, unless one of three Alarm Clocks re-runs it.
- On a free turn she can enter an optional race from the fixed calendar (G1 to OP), for SP, fans and stats.
- Fan totals level up her unique skill in Senior. Several scenarios ask for 60k fans by early February, 70k by early
  April and 120k by late December.

**Skills.** SP come from training, races and events, and skills can be bought at any time. Each **hint** takes about
10% off its skill's cost, up to 40%.

**Bonds.**
- A deck is six cards: five of the player's own and one borrowed. Each turn, each card shows up at one facility or at
  none.
- A card's bond gauge runs 0–100. It gains +7 for a training together, +5 for a hint and +5–10 for the card's events.
- At **80** the gauge turns orange. From then on, the card at the facility of its own type triggers **friendship
  training**, a large multiplier. Several at once, called "rainbows", make a run.

**Events.** Random training events, the trainee's own story, each card's chain of 2–3 events with choices, and fixed
dates.

**Inheritance.**
- The player picks two parents, and each brings her own two parents.
- Their **sparks** are blue (stats), pink (aptitudes), green (unique skill) and white (skills, races, scenarios), each
  rated 1–3★.
- Blue sparks add starting stats. In early April of Classic and of Senior, each spark rolls for stats, aptitudes or
  hints.
- The finished trainee becomes a **Veteran** with freshly rolled sparks, usable as a parent.

**Stat caps.**
- Stats run up to 2,000 (1,200 at launch), and races count anything above 1,200 at half.
- Each scenario sets its own cap for each stat: URA 1,400 for every stat (Global), L'Arc 1,600 / 1,600 / 1,500 /
  1,500 / 1,300. Beyond Dreams lifts Speed to 2,100.

**The final rating.**
- Stats pay rating points on a rising curve: about 2,600 at 1,000 and 3,800 at 1,200.
- Each skill adds a flat amount, and each unique-skill level adds 120–170.
- Rank **A** starts at 10,000. The ladder runs G → SS+ → UG … US, and Beyond Dreams added ranks above US9.

## 3. How uma-racer models scenarios today

- **The fifteen originals in JP order,** in three tiers of five ([scenarios](../../game/scenarios.md)).
- **No calendar and no turns.** A scenario is 10 Racing Levels earned only by racing, plus six goals: a G3, two G2s
  and three G1s ([03](../../game/03-uma-progression.md) §4). They can be won in any order with free retries: the
  original's objectives without dates or failure.
- **Stats are unlocked, not trained** ([05](../../game/05-training-hub.md)). Four facilities split up what a career
  does in the original, 107 unlocks a tier:

  | Facility | What it unlocks | Its counterpart in the original |
  |---|---|---|
  | Observatory | Constellation card nodes | Training with cards |
  | Lineage Hall | Lineage slots, uniques, golds | Inheritance |
  | Gym | Extra Stats, paid with Farm meals | Stat gains |
  | Dojo | A timed, yes-or-no bond per card | The bond gauge |

- **What makes a scenario different:** a cap budget (6,000 to 9,500), an SP cap, one buff on card nodes, a
  constellation shape, a card-era window and its rival field ([04](../../game/04-tiers-and-promotion.md) §8). None of
  the original gimmicks is played.
- **Not modelled from the original:** energy, mood, events, friendship training (removed by D326), camps, finales (a
  World Expedition per tier is planned instead), and the final rating.
- **The clock sets the pace:** timed Farm jobs and sessions, skipped with Speed Tickets. 25 Umas max a scenario in about
  4 weeks ([12](../../game/12-economy-and-pacing.md) §5). The drawing board removes the clock, and the Training Hub's
  inputs go with it.

## 4. A candidate mini-game per scenario

**What stages pay** (names provisional). Each pays into the racing build:
- **Stat points**, up to the scenario's caps.
- **SP and hints**, for skills.
- **Bond**, per card, toward the original's 80.
- **Sparks**, for lineage.
- **Aptitude points**, for distance, surface and style.
- **Boosts**: one-use multipliers counted in stages, never in minutes.

All stages are portrait and one-thumb, 3–7 minutes long, and seeded like the race engine.

**Kinds:** *T* is skill or timing; *S* is choices or strategy.

| # | Mini-game | Kind | Echoes | Pays |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stride Meter**: release in the zone, over three heats with a tighter zone each time | T | Three finale races | All five stats |
| 2 | **Team Clash**: the old Uma Puzzles orb battle, a party of five against a rival team | T+S | Team showdowns; bursts | Bond; hints |
| 3 | **Circuit Planner**: pick a mini-season of races and buy items, weighing fatigue against a points target | S | Free schedule; the shop | SP and hints |
| 4 | **Encore**: a three-lane rhythm chart | T | Songs and lives | Speed and Guts; SP |
| 5 | **Fragment Forge**: a 4×4 merge board in three colours | S | Fragments to Wisdom | Chosen stats; a colour bonus |
| 6 | **Overseas Run**: a steering runner over foreign courses | T | The France trip | Aptitude points; Stamina and Power |
| 7 | **Festival Decathlon**: 5-second micro-sports; streaks trigger a heat-up | T | 15 sports | Each sport's stat; SP |
| 8 | **Tasting Table**: deduce hidden judge tastes from five vegetables | S | Tastings | Boosts; stats |
| 9 | **Core Tuning**: rotate circuit tiles to power three cores | S | Chips and cores | Speed and Power |
| 10 | **Legend's Deck**: a three-fight deckbuilder, picking one of three knowledges after each fight | S | Legends; roguelike | Gold hints; Power and Guts |
| 11 | **Island Plan**: place facilities for adjacency points | S | Build order | Stamina and Wit; stage bonuses |
| 12 | **Spring Driller**: dig through layers, switching tools | T | Digging | Stamina and Guts; Bath boosts |
| 13 | **Dream Team**: assign three teammates to tasks, where the weakest sets the score | S | Rank = lowest member | Speed; SP |
| 14 | **Ramen Rush**: assemble orders against tickets | T | Ramen; regions | SP; the region's stat |
| 15 | **Five-Colour Rite**: a call-and-response sequence; teachings on cards decide which colours pay | T | Rites; teachings | Bond; Wit |

**Where the retired games fit:**
- **Uma Puzzles belongs in Unity Cup.** It was already a five-Uma party battle (D267–D290), and a team of five
  against rival teams is Unity Cup's identity.
- **The Twinkle Legends** suits the retired Friendship Training roguelite (D146).

**Each tier gets a mix:**

| Tier | Timing (T) | Strategy (S) | Both |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| Tier 2 | 2 | 3 | |
| Tier 3 | 3 | 2 | |

**Five engines can carry all fifteen:**
- a drag board: 2 and 5;
- a timing lane: 1, 4 and 14;
- a tile grid: 8, 9 and 11;
- cards and choices: 3, 10 and 13;
- an action scroller: 6, 7, 12 and 15.

If fewer genres are wanted, the ten most distinct are 1–6, 10, 11, 12 and 14.

## 5. Calendar pacing without real time

**The anchor.** The original's 72 turns take 20–40 minutes because each turn resolves at once. If every turn becomes a
stage, a career grows tenfold.

**The assumptions:**
- A stage takes 3–7 minutes, 5 on average.
- Six goal races take about 1 minute each.
- The owner plays 1 hour a day (7 hours a week): an assumption.

| Model | What is played | Arithmetic | One career |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Every turn a stage** (rejected) | 72 stages | 72 × 3–7 min | **3.6–8.4 h** (6 h typical) |
| **B. Season stages** | 12 seasons + 1 finale = 13 stages. Each season's six turns are one instant plan pick (about 30 s) | 13 × 3–7 = 39–91 min, + 6 min of picks + 6 min of races | **51–103 min** (≈75) |
| **C. Key turns only** | 72 instant command turns at about 12 s each (14 min), plus 8 stages: five half-year checks, 2 camps, 1 finale | 14 + (24 to 56) + 6 min | **44–76 min** (≈60) |
| **D. Month stages** | 36 + 1 = 37 stages | 37 × 3–7 = 111–259 min | **1.9–4.3 h** (≈3 h) |

**Across the roster (25 Umas, 15 scenarios).** One wave is every Uma through one scenario.

| Model | One wave | At 7 h a week | The whole game (15 waves) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 25 × 6 h = 150 h | 21 weeks | 315 weeks |
| B | 25 × 75 min = 31 h | 4.5 weeks | 67 weeks |
| C | 25 × 60 min = 25 h | 3.6 weeks | 54 weeks |
| D | 25 × 3 h = 77 h | 11 weeks | 165 weeks |

**What the arithmetic shows:**
- **B and C keep today's pace without a timer.** Today's measured pace is about 3–4 weeks a scenario and about 52 weeks
  for the whole game ([12](../../game/12-economy-and-pacing.md) §10). C matches it; B runs about a quarter longer.
- **B** is mini-game-led: stages are about 85% of play.
- **C** is the most faithful: energy, mood and rest return as turn rules, not time rules, and stages are about
  two-thirds of play.
- **D** fits only a roster of about 8 Umas.
- Replaying a stage for a better grade adds 20–30%.
